# TestAS FAQ: Everything Students and Parents Need to Know

TestAS is a standardized cognitive aptitude examination for international students aspiring to study at German universities. The exam measures where your academic reasoning skills rank compared to international peers. Leading universities in Germany and German-standard institutions in Vietnam—such as [Vietnamese-German University (VGU)](/en/blog/xet-tuyen-vgu-bang-testas)—rely on these results for preferential admissions.

![Everything Students and Parents Need to Know About TestAS](/images/blog/nhung-dieu-hoc-sinh-phu-huynh-can-biet-ve-testas.webp)

---

## 30-Second Quick Summary

* **Exam Purpose:** Assesses academic readiness for German higher education; there is no pass/fail grading.
* **Paper Exam Structure:** Consists of the compulsory Core Test (Kerntest) and an elective Subject-Specific Module Test.
* **Language Medium:** You freely choose between English or German; both hold equal validity.
* **Age & Attempts:** No age limits apply; you may retake the exam as many times as desired.
* **Score Validity:** TestAS certificates are valid indefinitely (never expire).
* **Where to Register:** Directly on the official examination portal at [www.testas.de](https://www.testas.de).

## Group 1: Exam Structure and Language Options

### 1. Which TestAS formats are offered?
TestAS is administered in two distinct testing formats:
* **Digital TestAS (Computer-based):** Conducted at certified digital test rooms featuring 6 specialized subject modules.
* **Paper-based TestAS (Written):** The traditional paper-and-pencil multiple-choice format featuring 4 subject modules.

*Read our detailed format breakdown:* [Digital TestAS vs. Paper TestAS: Detailed Format Comparison](/en/blog/digital-testas-la-gi-so-sanh-paper-va-digital).

### 2. What sections constitute the Paper-based TestAS?
The written exam consists of two consecutive test sections:

![Structure of Paper-based TestAS: Core Test and Subject Modules](/images/blog/bai-thi-testas-tren-giay-gom-nhung-phan-nao.webp)

* **Core Test (Kerntest):** Mandatory for all examinees. The test lasts **110 minutes** across 4 subtests: Solving Quantitative Problems, Inferring Relations, Completing Number Series, and Continuing Numerical Patterns.
* **Subject-Specific Module:** Lasts between **145 and 150 minutes**. Examinees select exactly 1 of the following 4 academic fields:
  * Humanities, Cultural Studies, and Social Sciences.
  * Engineering.
  * Mathematics, Computer Science, and Natural Sciences.
  * Economics.

If applying for a German student visa or university admission, completing a subject module is compulsory. In any single exam date, you can only register for one subject module.

*Read our comprehensive field selection guide:* [How to Choose Your TestAS Subject Module for VGU and Germany](/en/blog/huong-dan-chon-khoi-nganh-thi-testas).

### 3. Should you take the exam in English or German?
You can select either German or English when registering:

* Your language choice does not affect how your score is calculated or scaled.
* The test language carries no negative implications for any future academic admissions.
* Choose the language in which you read, comprehend, and reason fastest under time pressure.
* TestAS tests logical deduction rather than language mastery. If your German is below a firm B2 level, taking the exam in English prevents losing valuable minutes to vocabulary hurdles.

### 4. How can you effectively prepare for TestAS?
Because TestAS evaluates cognitive logic and reasoning rather than memorized academic facts, rote memorization does not work.

However, you can markedly improve your score through format familiarization and strategy training:
* Review official sample exams in German and English on [testas.de](https://www.testas.de).
* Master pacing techniques and practice elimination strategies for quantitative reasoning problems.
* Check our complete preparation roadmap: [TestAS Preparation Guide from A–Z: Roadmap and Time Allocation](/en/blog/kinh-nghiem-thi-testas).

## Group 2: Eligibility, Retakes, and Test Centres in Vietnam

### 5. Are there age restrictions for taking TestAS?
There are **no age restrictions**. Students in Grade 11 or Grade 12 are fully eligible to sit the exam. Taking TestAS in Grade 11 is an optimal strategy to alleviate senior-year graduation stress.

### 6. How many times can you take TestAS?
You may sit the exam **unlimited times**. If your initial score does not satisfy your expectations, you can register for subsequent dates and submit the highest scorecard.

### 7. Where can you take TestAS in Vietnam?
Accredited examination centres in Vietnam include:
* **Hanoi:** Vietnamese-German Centre (VDZ), Hanoi University of Science and Technology (HUST).
* **Ho Chi Minh City:** Goethe-Institut Ho Chi Minh City (Deutsches Haus, 33 Le Duan Boulevard, District 1).
* **Binh Duong:** Vietnamese-German University (VGU) for university-administered test sessions.

## Group 3: Registration, Examination Fees, and Score Reports

### 8. Where do you register for TestAS?
* Create an account and register online at: [www.testas.de](https://www.testas.de).
* Track active registration windows and session dates at: [www.testas.de/en/pruefungstermine_en.htm](https://www.testas.de/en/pruefungstermine_en.htm).

### 9. How do you pay the examination fee?
* Pay online using an international credit or debit card.
* The portal accepts Mastercard and Visa enabled for e-commerce transactions.
* The standard international registration fee is **150 Euro** per session.

### 10. When are scores released and how long are they valid?
* Approximately **4 weeks** after test day, scores become available in your online account at www.testas.de. You can download an official PDF score certificate with electronic verification codes.
* TestAS certificates are **valid indefinitely (lifetime validity)**. Your scores never expire, eliminating the renewal pressure typical of language exams.

## Group 4: Interpreting Your TestAS Score Report

TestAS does not assign pass or fail grades. Universities evaluate applicants using standardized psychometric benchmarks:

![How to Interpret TestAS Score Reports and Standard Score Ranges](/images/blog/cach-doc-hieu-diem-so-testas.webp)

1. **Percentile Rank (Prozentrang):** 
   * Indicates the percentage of global test-takers who scored equal to or lower than you.
   * *Example:* A Percentile Rank of 60 means your performance equals or exceeds 60% of all examinees taking that session worldwide.
2. **Standard Score (Standardwert):** 
   * A normalized metric designed to calibrate performance across diverse testing dates.
   * The scale spans from **70 to 130 points**, with **100 points** representing the international mean benchmark.

### Standard Score (Standardwert) Interpretation Matrix

| Standard Score Range | Performance Tier | Comparative Meaning Worldwide |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Below 90 points** | Below Average | Score falls below the average norm of participating global examinees. |
| **90 to 110 points** | Average Benchmark | Represents the mainstream range for the majority of international test-takers (100 is benchmark average). |
| **Above 110 points (111–130)** | Above Average / Outstanding | Demonstrates superior cognitive reasoning, placing you among top-tier academic candidates. |
